How to read the results

Patterns, not conclusions. Human Touch describes patterns in a text. It does not determine who wrote it or how, and no tool — including expensive commercial ones — can. A result is a reason to look closer and ask questions, never a finding about a person.

Human writing can look AI-like. Formal, polished or very simple writing can show AI-like patterns. So can writing by people who learned English as a second language, and simple children's prose. Never use a result on its own to make a decision about anyone, and never make public claims about a person or their work based on it.

Four kinds of patterns. Reads as human-written: the patterns typical of human writing. Signs of AI-assisted editing: specific, personal substance with an unusually smooth, polished surface. Signs of an edited AI draft: a template-like structure with personal touches layered on. Strong AI-like patterns: generic content and surface typical of unedited AI output. The middle two are the hardest to tell apart, so read the percentages, not just the label. Many people use AI tools legitimately — for spelling, translation or editing — so AI-like patterns are not evidence of wrongdoing.

Human writing has fingerprints: uneven rhythm, specific memories, names, places and numbers, opinions, small imperfections, and a voice that takes a side. AI writing tends to be smooth, balanced, general, and evenly paced.

The stories our children read shape who they become.

Children learn empathy, courage and what it means to be human from the books in their hands. As AI writes more of what we read, families deserve tools to look closer — and to recognise the signs of a real person, with real feelings, judgement and a point of view, behind the words.

Human Touch doesn't oppose AI. It makes authorship visible, so parents, teachers and readers can choose with their eyes open.
