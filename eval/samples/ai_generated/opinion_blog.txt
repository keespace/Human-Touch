# Hold the Phone: Why Kids Should Wait Until 13

Handing a child a smartphone has become a rite of passage, often arriving around age ten. Parents cite safety, convenience, and the fear that their kid will be left out. These concerns are real, but the evidence increasingly suggests we're giving children powerful, addictive devices before they're ready to manage them.

Consider what a smartphone actually is. It's not just a phone. It's a portal to social media platforms engineered by some of the world's brightest minds to capture attention and maximize engagement. Adults struggle to resist the endless scroll. Expecting a nine-year-old, whose impulse control is still developing, to self-regulate is unrealistic.

The timing matters, too. Early adolescence is a period of intense social comparison and emotional vulnerability. Researchers like Jonathan Haidt have linked the rise of smartphones and social media in the early 2010s to sharp increases in anxiety, depression, and self-harm among young people, especially girls. Researchers still debate how much of this is causation and how much is correlation, but the trend is troubling enough to warrant caution.

Then there's what smartphones displace: sleep, outdoor play, face-to-face friendships, boredom that sparks creativity, and the unstructured time in which children learn independence and resilience.

None of this means kids must be cut off entirely. The safety argument has merit, and a basic phone that calls and texts addresses it without the risks. Families can also share a tablet with clear limits, or allow supervised computer use for schoolwork and staying in touch.

The "everyone else has one" problem is best solved collectively. When groups of parents agree to delay together, no child is isolated. Movements like "Wait Until 8th" show this is possible.

Childhood is short. Smartphones will be waiting. Let's give kids a few more years to grow up first.
