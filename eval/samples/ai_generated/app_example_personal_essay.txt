My grandmother kept her recipes in a biscuit tin that smelled faintly of cardamom and rust. I found it the week after the funeral, wedged behind the rice cooker she never once used. Most of the cards were in Tamil. A few were in English, and those were the ones that made me laugh, because she'd written things like "add salt until it tastes right, not like your uncle's cooking."

I tried the lemon rice on a Tuesday. It was wrong. Too sour, the mustard seeds burnt, and I'd forgotten the curry leaves entirely — which, honestly, she would have noticed from across the room. My daughter ate it anyway. She's seven and she said it tasted "like the old house," which I don't think is true, but I didn't correct her.

I've made it eleven times since. It's still not right. Maybe it never will be, and maybe that's fine. Some things you're supposed to keep chasing.
