# Hazel Sings Out

Deep in Whispering Wood, where the moss grew soft and green, lived a little hedgehog named Hazel.

Every evening, Hazel tiptoed to the old oak stump to listen to the Forest Choir. The robins trilled. The frogs went *ribbit-rum*. The owls hooted low and deep.

Hazel hummed along, very quietly, from behind a fern.

"I wish I could sing with them," she whispered. But whenever she thought about stepping out, her tummy wobbled and her prickles stood straight up. *Boing!*

One night, Mrs. Badger, the choir leader, tapped her stick. "Oh dear," she sighed. "Our lullaby needs one more voice, a small, gentle one. Who could it be?"

Hazel's heart went *thump-thump*. She curled into a tight, prickly ball.

But then she remembered her grandmother's words: *"Even the smallest acorn grows into a tree."*

Slowly, Hazel uncurled. One paw. Two paws. One trembling nose.

"I... I could try," she squeaked.

Everyone turned to look. Hazel's prickles quivered. She closed her eyes and began to sing.

Her voice was soft as falling snow and sweet as clover honey. It floated over the stump, through the branches, and up to the twinkling stars.

When she finished, the wood was silent.

Then the robins cheered. The frogs thumped. The owls hooted, "Whoo-hoo-HOORAY!"

"That," said Mrs. Badger, smiling, "is exactly the voice we needed."

From that night on, Hazel sang with the Forest Choir. Her prickles still wobbled a little sometimes.

But she sang anyway.

*The End*
